You are scoring the readability and usefulness of a fraud triage brief's
narrative, on a 1–5 rubric. An analyst has fifteen seconds and a queue of 200
cases.

Score the narrative only. Ignore whether the recommendation is correct.

**5 — Excellent.** Leads with the finding. Names the two or three signals that
actually drive the case and how they interact. States the uncertainty plainly if
there is any. An analyst could act on this without opening the evidence panel.

**4 — Good.** Accurate and readable, leads with the finding, but either buries
one relevant signal or spends a sentence on something that does not change the
decision.

**3 — Adequate.** All the facts are there but the reader has to assemble them.
Reads as a list rendered into sentences. No clear lead.

**2 — Poor.** Restates the transaction and the score without interpretation, or
hedges so thoroughly that no decision follows from it, or opens with filler
("Based on the evidence provided...").

**1 — Unusable.** Contradicts itself, is unreadable, or says nothing an analyst
did not already know from the score alone.

Penalise, at one point each:

- preamble before the finding
- listing every signal instead of the decisive ones
- confident tone on a case the brief itself flags as incomplete

Do not reward length. A 3-sentence brief that names the decisive signals scores
above a 5-sentence brief that covers everything evenly.
