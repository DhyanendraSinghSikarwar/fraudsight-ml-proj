# Human labelling protocol — judge calibration set

**Purpose.** The LLM judges in `src/fraudsight/evals/judges.py` produce two of the
headline numbers in the README: the hallucination rate and the mean narrative
score. Neither is trustworthy until we know how well the judge agrees with a
person. This protocol produces the 30 human labels that Cohen's kappa is
computed against.

**Current status: not yet done.** The repository ships
`evals/datasets/reference_labels.jsonl`, which is generated by a deterministic
rubric, and every metric derived from it is labelled `judge_vs_rules` rather
than `judge_vs_human`. That gap is recorded in [docs/scope.md](../docs/scope.md).
Producing the file below closes it, and the code path already prefers
`human_labels.jsonl` whenever it exists.

Budget about 90 minutes.

---

## Procedure

```bash
uv run fraudsight label --n 30 --out evals/datasets/human_labels.jsonl
```

The command shows one case at a time: the evidence bundle, then the brief.
Score each without looking at the confirmed outcome — you are judging the brief
against its evidence, not against reality. Knowing the answer changes how
generous a reader is about groundedness, which is the exact bias the judge is
being tested for.

Label two things per case.

### 1. Groundedness — binary

**1 (grounded)** — every factual claim in the evidence list and the narrative is
present in, or directly follows from, the evidence bundle.

**0 (not grounded)** — at least one claim states something the bundle does not
contain.

Rules for the edge cases, so two labellers agree:

- A number that appears nowhere in the bundle → **0**, even if the sentence
  around it is accurate.
- Rounding, unit changes, and rephrasing → **1**. `$1,240.33` cited as "about
  $1,240" is grounded.
- General domain knowledge stated as general knowledge ("round micro-amounts are
  a card-testing pattern") → **1**. The same knowledge stated as a fact about
  *this* transaction ("this card was tested at four other merchants") → **0**
  unless the bundle says so.
- "No device record was attached" when the device tool returned `found: false`
  → **1**. Absence of evidence is a legitimate observation.
- A cited `case_id` that is not in the retrieved set → **0**.

### 2. Narrative quality — 1 to 5

Use the rubric in
[`src/fraudsight/triage/prompts/judge_narrative.v1.md`](../src/fraudsight/triage/prompts/judge_narrative.v1.md)
verbatim. It is the same text the LLM judge is given, which is the point: if the
two disagree, the disagreement is about judgement rather than about instructions.

Apply the three penalties strictly, one point each:

- preamble before the finding
- listing every signal instead of the decisive ones
- confident tone on a case the brief itself flags as incomplete

Do not reward length.

## Sampling

The command samples 30 cases stratified 10 / 10 / 10 across
`confirmed_fraud`, `confirmed_legitimate`, and `ambiguous`. Do not re-sample to
get a nicer spread of scores — a calibration set chosen for its labels is not a
calibration set.

## What good looks like

- Groundedness kappa ≥ 0.60 — below this the judge is not usable and the eval
  gate fails on the instrument.
- Narrative kappa (quadratic-weighted) ≥ 0.60. Expect this one to be lower; a
  1–5 taste judgement is genuinely harder to agree on than a binary fact check.

If kappa comes in low, the fix is the **rubric**, not the labels. Ambiguity in
the rubric is what produces disagreement, and re-labelling until the number
improves is how a calibration set becomes meaningless.

## Re-labelling

Redo this whenever `judge_groundedness.*.md` or `judge_narrative.*.md` changes.
The prompt hash is recorded in every eval report header, so a stale calibration
is detectable: if `judge_calibration.json` names a prompt hash the current report
does not, the calibration no longer applies to the judge in use.
